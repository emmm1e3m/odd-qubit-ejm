\section{Discussion}
\label{discussion:section}

The circuit decomposition in Sec.~\ref{physics:implementation} relates the local polarization to a phase-gate angle. Along the unified family, the four-feature kernel cancels the background contribution to each local Bloch component in Eq.~\eqref{core:signal}. The common radius is then determined entirely by interference between the background state and the phase correction on the all-zero string. In terms of the zero-string gate angle $\theta=\pi/2-t$, the radius is $r_n(t)=r_E(n)(1-\cos\theta)$ and increases from zero to twice the reference value as $\theta$ varies from zero to $\pi$.

Whether twice the reference radius is optimal depends on the class of phase paths. In the fixed phase representation, consider straight paths through the specified reference measurement that preserve regular tetrahedra of common radius in its neighborhood. For $n\ge5$, the largest radius attainable with a two-valued real generator is $2r_E(n)$ (Theorem~\ref{bound:theorem}). The four-qubit case illustrates how the shape of the path affects this range. A curved path through the same reference measurement exceeds the radius bound for all admissible straight paths at that size (Theorem~\ref{low:theorem}). For $n\ge5$, it remains open whether the same geometric constraints bound the radius by $2r_E(n)$ along curved paths or along straight paths with arbitrary real generators.

Throughout the radius interval attained by the unified family, the one-qubit cuts minimize the concurrence, so the local radius also determines the genuine multipartite concurrence of each basis state (Sec.~\ref{physics:entanglement}). In ideal entanglement swapping, this relation carries over to the conditional state at the remote sites~\cite{Zukowski1993,Huang2022}. Suppose a central station holds one qubit from each of $n$ Bell pairs. A central measurement outcome corresponding to the basis state $\ket{\psi_k}$ leaves the remote qubits in the unnormalized state
\begin{equation}
 (\bra{\psi_k}_C\otimes I_L)\ket{\Phi^+}_{CL}^{\otimes n}
 =2^{-n/2}\ket{\psi_k^*}_L.
 \label{discussion:swapping}
\end{equation}
Each outcome $k$ occurs with probability $2^{-n}$. After the local Pauli corrections of Eq.~\eqref{core:orbit} conditioned on the announced outcome, the remote state is the conjugate seed $\ket{\psi_0^*}$ and has concurrence $\sqrt{1-r_n(t)^2}$.

At the zero-radius endpoints of the two families, the same local polarization is compatible with different multipartite entanglement. For every even $n\ge6$, each remote qubit at either endpoint is maximally mixed, so every single-site measurement has the same distribution at both endpoints. Consider measuring $Y$ at site 1 and $Z$ at site 2. The unified and cosine measurement seeds have $\langle Y_1 Z_2\rangle=1/2$ and $1$, respectively (Appendix~\ref{physics:app-invariants}). Since $Y^*=-Y$, these correlations change sign in the corrected remote states. The two sites thus obtain opposite signs with probability $3/4$ at the unified endpoint and $1$ at the cosine endpoint.
