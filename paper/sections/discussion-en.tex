\section{Discussion}
\label{discussion:section}

The difference between the two zero-radius endpoints of Sec.~\ref{physics:entanglement} can be observed at separate sites in an ideal entanglement-swapping experiment~\cite{Zukowski1993,Huang2022}. Suppose a central station holds one qubit from each of $n$ Bell pairs. Projecting its qubits onto a measurement state $\ket{\psi_k}$ leaves the remote qubits in the unnormalized state
\begin{equation}
 (\bra{\psi_k}_C\otimes I_L)\ket{\Phi^+}_{CL}^{\otimes n}
 =2^{-n/2}\ket{\psi_k^*}_L.
 \label{discussion:swapping}
\end{equation}
The squared norm gives probability $2^{-n}$ for each outcome $k$. Once the central station announces $k$, the remote sites can apply the local Pauli corrections from Eq.~\eqref{core:orbit} to recover the conjugate seed $\ket{\psi_0^*}$. Choosing the central measurement along the unified family thus tunes the remote state's local polarization and its concurrence $\sqrt{1-r_n(t)^2}$.

For even $n\ge6$, let the central station use either zero-radius endpoint and, after the Pauli correction, let sites 1 and 2 measure $Y$ and $Z$, respectively. Each site has marginal $I/2$ and records either sign with equal probability, but the two signs are opposite with probability $3/4$ at the unified endpoint and $1$ at the cosine endpoint. Indeed, the measurement seeds have $\langle Y_1Z_2\rangle=1/2$ and $1$, respectively (Appendix~\ref{physics:app-invariants}), and complex conjugation reverses this correlation because $Y^*=-Y$. For any fixed central outcome, the correlation has the same magnitude before correction since a local Pauli can only change its sign.

Along the unified family, the four-feature kernel cancels the background term in each signed local Pauli expectation value in Eq.~\eqref{core:signal}, so the local polarization comes entirely from interference with the correction at the physical zero string. The zero-string gate angle $\theta=\pi/2-t$ thus controls the common radius through $r_n(t)/r_E(n)=1-\cos\theta$, from zero at $\theta=0$ to twice the reference radius at $\theta=\pi$ (Sec.~\ref{physics:implementation}).

Within the same fixed phase representation, consider paths through the specified reference measurement that preserve regular tetrahedra with a common radius at all sites. For $n\ge5$, Theorem~\ref{bound:theorem} gives $2r_E(n)$ as the largest radius attainable along straight paths with two-valued real generators. Can a curved path, or a straight path whose real generator takes more than two values, reach a larger radius?
