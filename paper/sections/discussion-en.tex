\section{Discussion}
\label{discussion:section}

The phase representation treats global orthogonality and local geometric constraints separately: changes in the diagonal phases always preserve the orthonormality of the complete measurement basis, while the local regular-tetrahedral conditions are determined by relations among the signed Pauli expectation values. Equation~\eqref{core:signal} further decomposes these expectation values into background terms and interference terms involving the correction at the physical zero string. The four-feature kernel of the unified construction cancels all background terms, leaving the interference terms to control local polarization and reducing the calculations at different system sizes to fixed-size cancellation identities. This explains why one construction applies to both odd and even numbers of qubits. Along the unified family, the zero-string gate angle $\theta=\pi/2-t$ in Sec.~\ref{physics:implementation} gives $r_n(t)/r_E(n)=1-\cos\theta$: the radius vanishes when $R_0(\theta)$ is the identity and reaches twice the reference value when $R_0(\pi)=I-2\ket{0^n}\bra{0^n}$ is a reflection. At the zero-radius endpoint, the kernel phase remains and the complete measurement unitary has Clifford-hierarchy level four. This continuous variation of the common Bloch radius also gives all measurement basis states the same entanglement-tuning law across every single-qubit-versus-rest cut: as the radius increases, the single-qubit purity increases and the corresponding bipartite entanglement entropy decreases.

The ability to increase the radius also depends on the shape of the phase path. At four qubits, even with arbitrary real generators, no admissible straight line increases the reference radius, whereas an explicit curved path through the same reference point does. Thus a classification of admissible straight lines does not exhaust the possible continuous deformations. The nonlinear coordination of phases needed to preserve the local geometry is central to the four-qubit construction that increases the radius.

Compatibility between different deformation directions imposes a further constraint. For even $n\ge8$, the new radius-increasing family and the known cosine family embed into a common two-dimensional measurement family. At six qubits, even when the four input classes are assigned independent phases and the zero-string phase is varied separately, this model retains only the two original branches near the reference point. Two deformations being individually admissible therefore does not suffice to make them independently tunable parameters of one family.

Table~\ref{tab:summary} distinguishes the dimension conditions for radius enhancement and for combining deformation directions: straight Boolean phase paths attain twice the reference radius from $n=5$ onward, while the enhanced and cosine directions form a two-parameter family for even $n\ge8$. The two thresholds differ, so $n=6$ admits radius enhancement but not the two-parameter extension in the four-group model, and $n=4$ admits enhancement only along a curved path.

Within the constructed family, the common radius determines the genuine multipartite concurrence because every weakest cut isolates one qubit. These correlations also have an entanglement-swapping interpretation~\cite{Zukowski1993,Huang2022}: if a central station holds one half of each of $n$ Bell pairs and projects onto $\ket{\psi_k(t)}$, the unnormalized remote state is
\begin{equation}
 (\bra{\psi_k(t)}_C\otimes I_L)\ket{\Phi^+}_{CL}^{\otimes n}
 =2^{-n/2}\ket{\psi_k(t)^*}_L.
 \label{discussion:swapping}
\end{equation}
Each outcome has probability $2^{-n}$, and the Pauli-orbit structure permits local Pauli corrections to a common conditional state. The remote state therefore has the same reduced-state invariants and entanglement measures studied here; for every $n\ge5$, it remains genuinely multipartite entangled, with concurrence $\sqrt{1-r_n(t)^2}$ and tunable local polarization.

These results raise questions about more general phase solution sets. First, at four qubits, Ref.~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026} gives another measurement family reaching radius $3r_E(4)$. That work already identifies the global relation between its even-qubit family and this four-qubit line as an open question. Appendix~\ref{low:app-invariant} proves that its specified parameter segment contains no measurement equivalent to our reference measurement under local unitaries, qubit permutations, and outcome relabeling. This does not determine the connectivity of the two families. Within the fixed phase representation, can the curved branch constructed here be connected to the known larger-radius family by a continuous path preserving regular tetrahedra with a common radius? This question concerns the measurements continuously reachable from the specified reference measurement and whether the two radius-increasing constructions belong to the same connected component.

Second, at six qubits, the requirement of a common phase within each group could be relaxed by assigning different additional phases to computational-basis components with the same $(q_A,q_B)$ label. Whether these additional degrees of freedom can compensate for the local-component differences generated by the two deformations acting together, and produce a two-dimensional measurement family containing both known branches, remains open. Resolving this question would determine whether the observed crossing is a consequence of requiring a common phase within each group or a compatibility obstruction that persists under more general phase deformations. A further question is whether curved phase paths or generators with more than two values, through the specified reference measurement in the fixed phase representation, can yield radii above $2r_E(n)$ for $n\ge5$.
