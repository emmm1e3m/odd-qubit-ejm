\section{Discussion}
\label{discussion:section}

The phase representation separates global orthogonality from local geometry. Unitarity guarantees a complete measurement, while Eq.~\eqref{core:signal} resolves each signed local component into a background expectation and interference with the correction at the physical zero string. The four-feature kernel cancels the background for all $n\ge5$, leaving this interference to control the local polarization. The dimension-dependent character sums reduce to the same finite set of cancellations, so the mechanism applies to both parities. At $t=\pi/2$ the point correction vanishes, whereas at $t=-\pi/2$ its interference doubles the reference signal. Through the common reduced spectrum in Eq.~\eqref{core:spectrum}, this phase control gives the same change in single-qubit-versus-rest entanglement at every qubit and for every measurement outcome: increasing the radius lowers that entanglement.

The shape of an admissible path also matters. The four-law classification constrains two-valued phase lines, while the $n=4$ result shows that even allowing arbitrary real line generators can leave an obstruction that a curved path overcomes. There the higher-order regularity conditions restrict the admissible line directions to two planes, and the enhancing path leaves these planes. Likewise, the coexistence of two phase directions does not by itself guarantee that they can be varied independently. In the even-qubit construction, this compatibility is determined by mixed background terms. For even $n\ge8$, at least two odd internal bits permit parity-preserving pair flips, and the remaining fixed-size sums cancel through the kernel. At $n=6$, the single odd internal bit leaves a term proportional to $\sin\alpha\sin\beta$ that obstructs direct superposition. The full four-color phase model retains only the two crossing branches near the reference point.

Two questions follow from this geometry. At $n=4$, the specified segment of the larger-radius family in Ref.~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026} contains no measurement equivalent to our reference EJM under local unitaries, qubit permutations, and outcome relabeling. Whether the curved branch constructed here connects to that family elsewhere in the regular-tetrahedral phase space remains unresolved. At $n=6$, a finer phase partition may allow a two-parameter family containing both branches; resolving this requires controlling additional mixed terms beyond those present in the four-color phase model.
