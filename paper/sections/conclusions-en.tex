\section{Conclusions}
\label{conclusions:section}

We have constructed a continuous family of elegant joint measurements for every integer $n\ge5$, with fixed local tetrahedral directions and common radius covering $[0,2r_E(n)]$ through the specified reference measurement. The upper endpoint is attained and optimal among all admissible straight phase paths with two-valued real generators in the fixed phase representation, and $n=5$ is the smallest system size allowing a radius increase in this model. A single four-feature kernel cancels the background contributions and thus gives continuous control of local polarization while preserving global orthogonality.

The available deformations also depend on the compatibility of phase directions and the shape of the path. For even $n\ge8$, the radius-increasing and known cosine deformations form the two coordinate sections of a common two-parameter measurement family. At $n=6$, the four-group phase model admits only the two crossing branches near the reference point. At $n=4$, a curved path increases the radius despite the obstruction for every admissible real straight phase path, showing that the straight-line classification does not exhaust continuous radius tuning.

For every $n\ge5$, the family remains genuinely multipartite entangled along the entire path, with its concurrence fixed by the common radius and its weakest cuts isolating a single qubit. The zero-radius endpoint is inequivalent to every stabilizer pure state under local unitaries and qubit permutations for all $n\ge5$, and to the cosine endpoint for every even $n\ge6$. The complete phase factorization recovers the known reference level $n+1$~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026} and gives levels $n$ and $4$ at the enhanced and zero-radius endpoints, respectively, with associated localization resource bounds. In entanglement swapping, this family prepares remote states with tunable local polarization and genuine multipartite entanglement.

The main mathematical results of this paper have been formally proved in Lean.
