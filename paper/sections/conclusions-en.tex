\section{Conclusions}
\label{conclusions:section}

For every $n\ge5$, we have constructed a family of elegant joint measurements whose common tetrahedral radius $r$ varies continuously from zero to twice the reference value at fixed vertex directions. Doubling $r$ doubles the trace distance between distinct one-qubit marginals at a given site. Every basis state remains genuinely $n$-partite entangled and attains the largest genuine multipartite concurrence allowed by its one-qubit marginals, $C_{\rm GME}=\sqrt{1-r^2}$.

The unified and cosine families pass through the same reference measurement. At zero radius, all one-qubit marginals are $I/2$, yet every basis state has $C_{\rm GME}=1$ in the unified family and $C_{\rm GME}<1$ in the cosine family. A two-site correlation distinguishes these endpoints in ideal entanglement swapping. For each fixed $n\ge5$, the concurrence gap persists at sufficiently small positive radii, where corresponding states have identical nonzero Bloch vectors at every site.

We have further characterized the attainable radii along straight and curved phase paths, established the dimension-dependent restrictions, and derived an explicit circuit decomposition of the unified measurement. The corresponding Clifford-hierarchy levels are $n+1$, $n$, and $4$ at the reference, enhanced, and zero-radius points, respectively. These results connect local measurement geometry, multipartite entanglement, and the implementation of tunable multiqubit joint measurements.
