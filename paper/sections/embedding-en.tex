\section{Embedding the even-qubit family}
\label{embed:section}

The unified construction and the earlier even-qubit family provide two phase directions through the same reference measurement. We now ask whether both directions can be varied independently while preserving the tetrahedral geometry. To compare them at the level of the complete measurement, first express the earlier family in the notation of Sec.~\ref{core:framework}. For even $n$, retain
$E_n=\{2,4,\ldots,n-2\}$ and define
\begin{equation}
 q_A(z)=\prod_{j\in E_n}(1-z_j),\qquad d_A(z)=q_A(z)-\delta_{z,0}.
 \label{embed:and-support}
\end{equation}
The diagonal $i^{f_0(z)}e^{i\alpha d_A(z)}$ is precisely the known even-qubit family~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}, with parameter correspondence $\alpha=\pi\tau/2$. The phase factors in its background terms depend only on the even internal coordinates. Every character $h$ in Table~\ref{core:pullback-table} contains a coordinate outside $E_n$, so a flip of that coordinate cancels the background expectation. Moreover, $q_A(a)=1$ for class I and $q_A(a)=0$ for class II. Equation~\eqref{core:signal} then gives the common signed component $c_n\cos\alpha$, and hence $r=r_E(n)\cos\alpha$ for $|\alpha|\le\pi/2$. For $n=2$, the set $E_2$ is empty, so the empty-product convention gives $q_A\equiv1$. Only class I occurs, and its background terms still cancel in pairs. For $n=4$, $E_4=\{2\}$ gives $q_A=1-z_2$, with the same cancellations and flip values. The cosine law therefore includes both low-dimensional cases.

For even $n\ge8$, this family and the unified radius-increasing family are coordinate sections of a two-parameter family. Write $q_B=q_n$ and take the Boolean phase generator $d_B=d_n$ from Eq.~\eqref{core:support}. Set
\begin{align}
 D_{\alpha,\beta}(z)&=i^{f_0(z)}e^{i\alpha d_A(z)+i\beta d_B(z)},\notag\\
 M_n(\alpha,\beta)&=C_nD_{\alpha,\beta}H^{\otimes n}.
 \label{embed:measurement}
\end{align}
Figure~\ref{fig:embedding}(a) shows that, for even $n\ge8$, the two phase directions vary independently within one family, with $r_n/r_E(n)=\cos\alpha-\sin\beta$ on the domain $\Omega$ defined below. The section $\beta=0$ recovers the earlier cosine family, whereas $\alpha=0$ recovers the unified radius-increasing family, both column by column; the full domain spans radii from zero to $2r_E(n)$. Panel (b) shows the six-qubit exception: in the four-group phase model specified below, only the two crossing one-parameter branches survive near the reference point.

\begin{theorem}[Two-parameter embedding]
\label{embed:theorem}
For every even $n\ge8$, Eq.~\eqref{embed:measurement} defines an orthonormal measurement for all real $\alpha,\beta$. All its signed seed components equal
\begin{equation}
 m_n(\alpha,\beta)=c_n(\cos\alpha-\sin\beta).
 \label{embed:component}
\end{equation}
On the domain
\begin{equation}
 \Omega=\left\{(\alpha,\beta): |\alpha|\le\frac\pi2,\quad
 -\frac\pi2\le\beta\le\frac\pi2-|\alpha|\right\},
 \label{embed:domain}
\end{equation}
the measurement has the fixed local tetrahedra of Eq.~\eqref{core:bloch-orbit}, with
\begin{align}
 r_n(\alpha,\beta)&=r_E(n)(\cos\alpha-\sin\beta),\notag\\
 r_n(\Omega)&=[0,2r_E(n)].
 \label{embed:radius}
\end{align}
Its $\beta=0$ section recovers the earlier even-qubit family column by column, and its $\alpha=0$ section recovers Theorem~\ref{core:unified-theorem} column by column. The two parameter directions are independent as ordered measurement projectors.
\end{theorem}

The exception at $n=6$ concerns only the two-dimensional extension; the unified radius-increasing family still exists at this size. The direct superposition in Eq.~\eqref{embed:measurement} has equal signed components if and only if $\sin\alpha\sin\beta=0$. Even if the inputs are grouped by the four values of $(q_A,q_B)$, with an independent phase for each group and for the physical zero string, this four-group phase model retains only the two original one-parameter branches near the reference point. Appendix~\ref{embed:appendix} gives the mixed-term calculation and the local classification.

The essential step in proving Theorem~\ref{embed:theorem} is cancellation of the joint background terms. Put $u=e^{i\alpha}$ and $v=e^{i\beta}$. The seed before applying $-iC_n$ is
\begin{equation}
 2^{-n/2}\left[\sum_z i^{|z|}u^{q_A(z)}v^{q_B(z)}\ket z
 +(i-uv)\ket{0^n}\right],
 \label{embed:seed}
\end{equation}
At every class-I flip, $(q_A(a),q_B(a))=(1,0)$; at every class-II flip, the pair is $(0,1)$. Their defect contributions therefore agree, because
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{Re}[-iu^{-1}(i-uv)]
 &=\cos\alpha-\sin\beta,\notag\\
 \operatorname{Re}[-v^{-1}(i-uv)]
 &=\cos\alpha-\sin\beta.
 \label{embed:defect}
\end{align}
Class I leaves $q_A$ unchanged, reducing its background term to the already vanishing $q_B$ term. In class II, the AND difference separates the background sum into the three sectors $q_A(z)-q_A(z\oplus a)=1,0,-1$. In the first sector all even internal bits vanish. For $n\ge8$, there are at least two odd internal bits, and any nonconstant character on this block cancels by a parity-preserving pair flip. Only six fixed four-bit sums remain; their coefficients vanish. Appendix~\ref{embed:appendix} supplies the full classification and the six finite identities. This establishes Eq.~\eqref{embed:component} for every even $n\ge8$, after which the orbit formula gives all reduced states.

Using the same parameter $t$ on either coordinate section, both families have the zero-string correction $i-e^{it}$ but assign different relative phases to the background amplitudes entering each local expectation. At $t=-\pi/2$, the correction equals $2i$ in both families; its real interference contribution vanishes for the cosine family and reaches $2c_n$ for the unified family. Since the background terms vanish in these two constructions, the bound $|i-e^{it}|\le2$ explains the attained enhancement. Theorem~\ref{bound:theorem} extends the bound to all admissible two-valued generators, including those with nonzero backgrounds.

On $\Omega$, $\sin\beta\le\cos\alpha$, while $\cos\alpha-\sin\beta\le2$; the $\alpha=0$ section attains every value between zero and two. The two axis identities follow directly from the diagonal entries in Eq.~\eqref{embed:measurement}. To check that the parameters survive passage to projectors, note that a diagonal infinitesimal input phase $A$ produces only column phases exactly when $AH^{\otimes n}=H^{\otimes n}B$ for a diagonal $B$. Every Hadamard entry is nonzero, so $A$ and $B$ must be the same scalar. The pairs $(d_A,d_B)$ take the values $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$ at $0,e_1,e_1\oplus e_2$, respectively. No nonzero linear combination of the two directions is scalar, so none can change only the column phases while leaving all measurement projectors fixed. The two parameter directions are therefore independent at the level of ordered projectors.
