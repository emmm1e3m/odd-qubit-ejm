\section{Introduction}
\label{intro:section}

An entangled projective measurement is constrained both by the orthogonality of its global basis states and by the geometry of their local reductions. The elegant joint measurement (EJM) brings these two features together: the local Bloch vectors occupy the vertices of a regular tetrahedron, while the corresponding global states form an orthonormal basis. Its four local directions coincide with the geometry of a qubit symmetric informationally complete (SIC) measurement~\cite{Renes2004}. Introduced in the study of quantum networks~\cite{Gisin2017,Gisin2019}, the EJM at $n=2$ belongs to a continuous family connecting it to a Bell-state measurement~\cite{Tavakoli2021}. Members of this family yield violations of bilocal Bell inequalities~\cite{Tavakoli2021} and full-network-nonlocality criteria~\cite{PozasKerstjens2022}, and have been implemented in photonic entanglement-swapping experiments~\cite{Huang2022}.

The same two-qubit EJM also arises from maximizing the equal pairwise distinguishability of single-qubit reductions, an approach that admits higher-dimensional bipartite constructions from SIC measurements~\cite{Czartowski2021}. More broadly, iso-entangled two-qubit bases have been classified~\cite{DelSanto2024}, and the extension of a pure state to a complete basis through local unitaries has been studied~\cite{Pimpel2023}. Classification by the shared entanglement needed for local implementation provides a further operational perspective~\cite{PauwelsLocalization2025}. For the present problem, varying the tetrahedral radius changes the single-qubit reduced spectrum and hence the entanglement across each single-qubit-versus-rest cut. Extending this control to more qubits requires the local geometries of all measurement outcomes to remain compatible with global orthogonality.

Pauli-orbit constructions provide a useful setting for this problem~\cite{PauwelsPauli2026,PauwelsEJM2026}. In the associated phase normal form, every diagonal unitary generates a complete orthonormal basis; the remaining constraints concern the local Bloch vectors. Reference~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026} gives an explicit reference EJM for every $n\ge2$, with common radius
\begin{equation}
 r_E(n)=\frac{\sqrt3}{2^{n-1}},
 \label{intro:reference-radius}
\end{equation}
and an even-qubit phase family with radius $r_E(n)\cos t$ on $|t|\le\pi/2$. This construction already identifies interference involving a single computational-basis phase correction as the source of the local signal. The odd-qubit analysis in that work leaves an explicit tunable family through the specified reference point open for $n\ge5$, and identifies a locally isolated reference point at $n=3$ from a numerical Jacobian rank. Other symmetric multiqubit constructions permit broader local geometries~\cite{Ding2025,He2026}; here the target remains one fixed regular tetrahedron at each qubit, with a tunable common radius across qubits and outcomes.

Here we construct an explicit continuous family with radius $r_E(n)(1-\sin t)$ for every integer $n\ge5$. A single Boolean kernel, depending on four binary features, applies to both odd and even numbers of qubits. The family recovers the reference measurement column by column at $t=0$ and continuously connects zero local polarization to twice the reference radius. This radius increase raises a natural question about its limit. We answer it for all two-valued real phase generators through the reference measurement by classifying the admissible radius laws. The doubled radius is optimal in this model, which includes generators beyond the four-feature construction.

The unified construction also makes it possible to compare the new radius-increasing deformation with the known even-qubit cosine deformation within one phase representation. We examine whether both can be varied independently while preserving the local tetrahedra. For every even $n\ge8$, the answer is an explicit two-parameter family of complete measurements containing the two deformations as exact coordinate sections. At $n=6$, mixed interference obstructs this extension; in the model that groups inputs by the four joint values of two Boolean functions and assigns one phase to each group, the local solutions consist of two crossing branches.

The lower-dimensional cases clarify why a straight phase path can cease to be sufficient. No straight phase path with a two-valued generator increases the reference radius for $n=2,3,4$. At $n=3$, we strengthen earlier numerical evidence~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026} with an analytic proof of local rigidity. At $n=4$, even allowing arbitrary real generators prevents every admissible straight line from increasing the radius, but an explicit curved path increases the radius to $(1+1/\sqrt2)r_E(4)$.
