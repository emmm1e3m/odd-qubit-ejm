\section{Introduction}
\label{intro:section}

The outcomes of a Bell-state measurement correspond to mutually orthogonal states, yet no single qubit distinguishes them: all four states have the same one-qubit marginals. In an elegant joint measurement (EJM), the marginals instead point to the vertices of a regular Bloch tetrahedron. Its directions have the geometry of a qubit symmetric informationally complete measurement~\cite{Renes2004}; its radius sets the trace distance between marginals at distinct vertices~\cite{Czartowski2021}. Enlarging the tetrahedron makes these local states easier to distinguish, while reducing the entanglement of each qubit with the rest. As the tetrahedra expand at fixed orientation, can the concurrence across the weakest bipartition of every state in a complete measurement basis still attain the upper bound set by its one-qubit marginals?

The two-qubit EJM arose in the study of quantum networks~\cite{Gisin2017,Gisin2019} and admits a continuous deformation in which its local tetrahedron shrinks until the Bell basis is reached~\cite{Tavakoli2021}. Members of this family have been used to study network nonlocality~\cite{PozasKerstjens2022} and implemented in photonic entanglement swapping~\cite{Huang2022}. Pauwels and Gisin obtained a reference EJM for every $n\ge2$, with radius
\begin{equation}
 r_E(n)=\frac{\sqrt3}{2^{n-1}},
 \label{intro:reference-radius}
\end{equation}
and found that the two-qubit cosine contraction law holds for every even $n$~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. This cosine path only contracts the tetrahedra, and for odd $n\ge5$ they left open how to deform the reference measurement in closed form. Other symmetric constructions allow different local geometries~\cite{Ding2025,He2026}, and a different four-qubit measurement with larger radius is known numerically~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. The question here is how to increase the radius continuously from the specified reference basis while keeping the tetrahedral directions fixed.

To construct such a deformation for every $n\ge5$, we use the Pauli-orbit representation~\cite{PauwelsPauli2026,PauwelsEJM2026}, in which varying the diagonal phases preserves orthogonality. Pauwels and Gisin traced the local polarization of the reference measurement to interference between a background state and a phase correction on a single computational-basis string~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. We use a Boolean function of four parity features to cancel the background contribution to every local Bloch component throughout the deformation, so that the remaining interference sets the radius for odd and even $n$ alike. The deformation recovers the reference basis state by state at $t=0$, and its common radius obeys $r_n(t)=r_E(n)(1-\sin t)$ for $-\pi/2\le t\le\pi/2$. At the enhanced endpoint this gives the same local radius as the reference basis on $n-1$ qubits.

What does this local control imply for entanglement across the whole system? A single-qubit marginal fixes the entanglement of that qubit with the rest, but says little on its own about cuts with several qubits on either side. In the four-qubit cosine family, for example, the endpoint is a product of two Bell pairs~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}, so each qubit is maximally mixed even though the pairs are unentangled with one another. For the family constructed here, we prove that the one-qubit cuts are precisely the bipartitions of least concurrence~\cite{Ma2011} for every basis state and every parameter value. Thus $C_{\rm GME}=\sqrt{1-r_n(t)^2}$ throughout, including at twice the reference radius. For even $n\ge6$, the cosine family lets us compare the entanglement across the weakest cuts at fixed local polarization. At radii sufficiently close to zero, corresponding states in the two families have the same one-qubit density matrix at every site but different entanglement across their weakest cuts.

The attainable radii depend on the choice of phase path. In the fixed phase representation, consider straight paths through the reference measurement that preserve regular tetrahedra of common radius in its neighborhood. For two-valued real generators, at least five qubits are needed for a radius increase, and twice the reference radius is optimal for every $n\ge5$. The restriction to straight paths matters at four qubits, where a curved path increases the radius even though no admissible real straight path does. For even $n\ge8$, the unified radius-increasing path and the cosine path are coordinate sections of one two-parameter family. The main mathematical results of this work have been formally proved in Lean.
