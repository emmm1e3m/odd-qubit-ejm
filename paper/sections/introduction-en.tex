\section{Introduction}
\label{intro:section}

The four basis states of a Bell-state measurement are mutually orthogonal. Their marginals on either qubit are identical, so a single-qubit measurement cannot distinguish them. In an elegant joint measurement (EJM), the marginals instead point to the vertices of a regular Bloch tetrahedron. The vertex directions coincide with those of a qubit symmetric informationally complete measurement~\cite{Renes2004}, and the radius sets the trace distance between marginals at distinct vertices~\cite{Czartowski2021}. Enlarging the tetrahedron makes these local states easier to distinguish, while reducing the entanglement of each qubit with the rest. For each basis state, the one-qubit marginals bound the concurrence across its weakest bipartition from above. We study whether every state in a complete basis can attain this bound as the tetrahedra expand at fixed orientation.

The two-qubit EJM arose in the study of quantum networks~\cite{Gisin2017,Gisin2019} and admits a continuous deformation in which its local tetrahedron shrinks until the Bell basis is reached~\cite{Tavakoli2021}. Members of this family have been used to study network nonlocality~\cite{PozasKerstjens2022} and implemented in photonic entanglement swapping~\cite{Huang2022}. Pauwels and Gisin obtained a reference EJM for every $n\ge2$, with radius
\begin{equation}
 r_E(n)=\frac{\sqrt3}{2^{n-1}},
 \label{intro:reference-radius}
\end{equation}
and found that the two-qubit cosine contraction law holds for every even $n$~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. The radius along this cosine path is bounded by the reference value. For odd $n\ge5$, that work left open the construction of an explicit continuous family through the corresponding reference measurement. Other symmetric constructions allow different local geometries~\cite{Ding2025,He2026}, and a different four-qubit measurement with larger radius is known numerically~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. We seek a continuous deformation that increases the radius from the specified reference basis while preserving the tetrahedral directions.

To obtain such a family for every $n\ge5$, we use the Pauli-orbit representation~\cite{PauwelsPauli2026,PauwelsEJM2026}, in which varying the diagonal phases preserves orthogonality. Pauwels and Gisin traced the local polarization of the reference measurement to interference between a background state and a phase correction on a single computational-basis string~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. We use a Boolean function of four parity features to cancel the background contribution to every local Bloch component throughout the deformation, so that the remaining interference sets the radius for odd and even $n$ alike. The deformation recovers the reference basis state by state at $t=0$, and its common radius obeys $r_n(t)=r_E(n)(1-\sin t)$ for $-\pi/2\le t\le\pi/2$. At the enhanced endpoint this gives the same local radius as the reference basis on $n-1$ qubits.

The spectrum of a one-qubit marginal determines the entanglement of that qubit with the rest. Determining the genuine multipartite concurrence of a basis state from this spectrum also requires a comparison with cuts that place several qubits on each side. In the four-qubit cosine family, for example, the endpoint is a product of two Bell pairs~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}, so each qubit is maximally mixed even though the pairs are unentangled with one another. For the family constructed here, we prove that the one-qubit cuts are precisely the bipartitions of least concurrence~\cite{Ma2011} for every basis state and every parameter value. Thus $C_{\rm GME}=\sqrt{1-r_n(t)^2}$ throughout, including at twice the reference radius. For even $n\ge6$, the cosine family lets us compare the entanglement across the weakest cuts at fixed local polarization. At radii sufficiently close to zero, corresponding states in the two families have the same one-qubit density matrix at every site but different entanglement across their weakest cuts.

The attainable radii depend on the choice of phase path. In the fixed phase representation, consider straight paths through the reference measurement that preserve regular tetrahedra of common radius in its neighborhood. For two-valued real generators, at least five qubits are needed for a radius increase, and twice the reference radius is optimal for every $n\ge5$. The restriction to straight paths matters at four qubits, where a curved path increases the radius even though no admissible real straight path does. For even $n\ge8$, the unified radius-increasing path and the cosine path are coordinate sections of one two-parameter family. The main mathematical results of this work have been formally proved in Lean.
