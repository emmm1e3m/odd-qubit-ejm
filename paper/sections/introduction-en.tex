\section{Introduction}
\label{intro:section}

The four Bell states are orthogonal but have identical marginals on either qubit, so no single-qubit measurement can distinguish them. In an elegant joint measurement (EJM), the local Bloch vectors point to the vertices of a regular tetrahedron. The vertex directions coincide with those of a qubit symmetric informationally complete measurement~\cite{Renes2004}, and the radius sets the trace distance between marginals at distinct vertices~\cite{Czartowski2021}. For two equally likely basis states with distinct marginals at a given site, doubling the radius doubles the optimal single-site discrimination advantage over random guessing. As the radius grows, each qubit becomes less entangled with the rest. This trade-off raises the question of how far the tetrahedra of a reference EJM can expand at fixed vertex directions and how much genuine multipartite entanglement each basis state can retain at a given radius.

The two-qubit EJM arose in the study of quantum networks~\cite{Gisin2017,Gisin2019} and admits a continuous deformation in which its local tetrahedron shrinks until the Bell basis is reached~\cite{Tavakoli2021}. Members of this family have been used to study network nonlocality~\cite{PozasKerstjens2022} and implemented in photonic entanglement swapping~\cite{Huang2022}. Pauwels and Gisin obtained a reference EJM for every $n\ge2$, with radius
\begin{equation}
 r_E(n)=\frac{\sqrt3}{2^{n-1}},
 \label{intro:reference-radius}
\end{equation}
and found that the two-qubit cosine contraction law holds for every even $n$~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. The radius along this cosine path is bounded by the reference value. For odd $n\ge5$, that work left open the construction of an explicit continuous family through the corresponding reference measurement. Other symmetric constructions allow different local geometries~\cite{Ding2025,He2026}, and a different four-qubit measurement with larger radius is known numerically~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}.

To increase the radius from this reference measurement, we use the Pauli-orbit representation~\cite{PauwelsPauli2026,PauwelsEJM2026}, in which varying the diagonal phases preserves orthogonality. Pauwels and Gisin traced the local polarization of the reference measurement to interference between a background state and a phase correction on a single computational-basis string~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. We use a Boolean function of four parity features to cancel the background contribution to every local Bloch component throughout the deformation, so that the remaining interference sets the radius for odd and even $n$ alike. For every $n\ge5$, the deformation recovers the reference basis state by state at $t=0$, and its common radius obeys $r_n(t)=r_E(n)(1-\sin t)$ for $-\pi/2\le t\le\pi/2$. At the enhanced endpoint the radius doubles, reaching the reference radius for $n-1$ qubits.

The genuine multipartite concurrence of a pure state is the minimum concurrence over its bipartitions~\cite{Ma2011}. A cut with several qubits on each side can have lower concurrence than any one-qubit cut. For example, the four-qubit cosine family ends in a product of two Bell pairs~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. Each qubit is maximally mixed, yet the pairs are unentangled with one another. For every basis state along our path, we prove that precisely the one-qubit cuts minimize the concurrence. Each state therefore attains the bound set by its one-qubit marginals, $C_{\rm GME}=\sqrt{1-r_n(t)^2}$, even at twice the reference radius. For comparison, we use the cosine family of Pauwels and Gisin for even $n$ and our cosine family for odd $n$. For each fixed $n\ge5$, at zero radius and at all sufficiently small positive radii, corresponding states have identical one-qubit marginals at every site, yet the unified family has strictly larger genuine multipartite concurrence.

The attainable radii also depend on the phase path. In the fixed phase representation, we consider straight paths through the reference measurement with two-valued real generators. We require the paths to preserve regular tetrahedra of common radius near that measurement. The largest radius on such paths is the reference radius for two to four qubits and twice that value for every $n\ge5$. At four qubits, a curved path can exceed the reference radius. For even $n\ge8$, the unified radius-increasing path and the cosine path are coordinate sections of one two-parameter family. The main mathematical results of this work have been formally proved in Lean.
