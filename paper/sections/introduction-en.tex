\section{Introduction}
\label{intro:section}

The outcomes of a Bell-state measurement correspond to mutually orthogonal states, yet no single qubit distinguishes them: all four states have the same one-qubit marginals. In an elegant joint measurement (EJM), the marginals instead point to the vertices of a regular Bloch tetrahedron. Its directions have the geometry of a qubit symmetric informationally complete measurement~\cite{Renes2004}; its radius sets the trace distance between marginals at distinct vertices~\cite{Czartowski2021}. Enlarging the tetrahedron makes these local states easier to distinguish, while reducing the entanglement of each qubit with the rest. As the tetrahedra expand at fixed orientation, can every state in a complete measurement basis attain the largest concurrence across its weakest bipartition allowed by its one-qubit marginals?

The two-qubit EJM arose in the study of quantum networks~\cite{Gisin2017,Gisin2019}. A continuous deformation shrinks its local tetrahedron until the Bell basis is reached~\cite{Tavakoli2021}. Members of this family have been used to study network nonlocality~\cite{PozasKerstjens2022} and implemented in photonic entanglement swapping~\cite{Huang2022}. Pauwels and Gisin obtained a reference EJM for every $n\ge2$, with radius
\begin{equation}
 r_E(n)=\frac{\sqrt3}{2^{n-1}},
 \label{intro:reference-radius}
\end{equation}
and found that the two-qubit cosine contraction law holds for every even $n$~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. They left open a tunable closed-form family through this reference measurement for odd $n\ge5$. Nor does their even-qubit cosine path increase the radius. Other symmetric constructions allow different local geometries~\cite{Ding2025,He2026}; a different four-qubit measurement with larger radius is also known numerically~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. What remains to be explained is how to expand the local tetrahedra continuously from the specified reference basis.

We construct such a deformation for every $n\ge5$. In the Pauli-orbit representation~\cite{PauwelsPauli2026,PauwelsEJM2026}, changing the diagonal phases preserves orthogonality. Pauwels and Gisin identified interference from a phase correction on a single computational-basis string as the source of the local polarization in the reference measurement~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. Building on this mechanism, we choose a Boolean function of four parity features that cancels the background contribution to every local Bloch component throughout the deformation. The remaining interference then controls the radius for both odd and even $n$. At $t=0$ the deformation recovers the reference basis state by state. For $-\pi/2\le t\le\pi/2$, the common radius is $r_n(t)=r_E(n)(1-\sin t)$. At the enhanced endpoint an $n$-qubit basis has the same local radius as the reference basis on $n-1$ qubits.

What does this local control imply for entanglement across the whole system? A single-qubit marginal fixes the entanglement of that qubit with the rest, but says little on its own about cuts with several qubits on either side. For example, the four-qubit cosine family ends in a product of two Bell pairs~\cite{PauwelsGisin2026}. Each qubit is then maximally mixed, although the pairs are unentangled with one another. For the family constructed here we prove that the weakest bipartitions, in the concurrence measure~\cite{Ma2011}, are precisely the one-qubit cuts for every basis state and every parameter value. Thus $C_{\rm GME}=\sqrt{1-r_n(t)^2}$ throughout: even the basis states with doubled local radius remain genuinely $n$-partite entangled.

We also determine how the attainable radii depend on the allowed phase paths. In the fixed phase representation, for $n\ge5$ twice the reference radius is optimal among straight phase paths generated by two-valued real functions that pass through the reference measurement and preserve regular tetrahedra of common radius in its neighborhood. Five is the smallest number of qubits for which this class permits an increase. Path shape matters: at four qubits a curved path increases the radius although no admissible real straight path does. For even $n\ge8$, the new and cosine paths are coordinate sections of one two-parameter family. The main mathematical results have also been formally proved in Lean.
